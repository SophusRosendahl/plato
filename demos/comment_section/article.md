# Sourdough for impatient people

Most sourdough guides assume you have a whole Saturday. You don't need one. You
need a lively starter, a fridge, and about fifteen minutes of hands-on time
spread over a day and a half.

## The starter

Feed your starter equal weights of flour and water. It is ready to use when it
has roughly doubled and smells pleasantly sour, usually 4 to 8 hours after a
feed at room temperature. If it collapses before you use it, feed it again.

## The schedule

- **Evening, day 1:** mix 500 g bread flour, 350 g water, 100 g active starter
  and 10 g salt. Squish it together until no dry flour remains. Cover.
- **Before bed:** two sets of stretch-and-folds, 30 minutes apart.
- **Overnight:** leave it on the counter. In a warm kitchen (above 24 °C),
  put it in the fridge after 3 hours instead.
- **Morning, day 2:** shape it into a tight ball, put it seam-side up in a
  floured bowl, and refrigerate until evening (8 to 12 hours).
- **Evening, day 2:** bake straight from the fridge in a preheated Dutch oven:
  20 minutes lid on at 250 °C, then 20 to 25 minutes lid off at 230 °C.

## Common problems

A flat loaf usually means the dough over-proofed: shorten the overnight rise.
A dense, gummy crumb usually means it under-proofed or was cut while hot: wait
an hour before slicing. A pale crust means the oven or pot wasn't hot enough.
